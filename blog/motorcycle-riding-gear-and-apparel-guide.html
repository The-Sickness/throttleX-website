Motorcycle Riding Gear and Apparel: A Complete Guide

By ThrottleX Team Published August 22, 2026 Read time 12 min

Riding gear is the one area of motorcycling where cutting corners has consequences that cannot be undone. A bad helmet, cheap gloves, or jeans instead of proper riding pants are decisions that feel reasonable until the moment they are not. This guide covers everything you need to know about each category of riding gear, what certifications mean, how to think about budget, and what to prioritize when you cannot afford everything at once.

// ATGATT

All The Gear, All The Time. You will hear this from experienced riders constantly. It is not about being paranoid. It is about the fact that crashes happen at the least expected moments and protective gear works exactly as well as you are wearing it when you need it.

HELMETS

The Helmet Critical: No Compromises

Your helmet is the single most important piece of protective gear you will buy. There is no category where budget shortcuts are less acceptable. In the US the DOT standard is the legal minimum. ECE 22.06 is the current European standard and is significantly more rigorous. SNELL certification is commonly required at track days. For street riding look for ECE 22.06 as a minimum.

Full-face helmets provide the most protection and are the correct choice for sport, track, and canyon riding. The helmet must fit snugly with no pressure points and must not move when you try to rotate it on your head. Replace it after any impact even with no visible damage — the EPS foam compresses permanently and cannot protect you again.

JACKETS

The Jacket Critical

A motorcycle jacket serves two functions: abrasion resistance to protect your skin in a slide, and impact protection at the shoulders, elbows, and back. Leather offers the best abrasion resistance and remains the standard for track riding. Textile jackets made from Cordura and Kevlar-blend fabrics offer excellent protection with better breathability.

The armor inside is graded by CE level. CE Level 2 armor is significantly better than Level 1 and is the standard to look for at the shoulders, elbows, and especially the back. Buy the Level 2 back protector. Try the jacket on while mimicking your riding posture — sleeves should not ride up when your arms are extended forward.

$150 to $300

GLOVES

Gloves Critical

Hands go down first in a crash. It is instinctive and unavoidable. Good gloves need palm sliders to distribute impact force, knuckle protection for direct impacts, and solid wrist closure to stay on in a crash. Cold hands lose sensitivity and slow your reaction time, making the right gloves for conditions a safety issue, not just comfort.

$50 to $120

// Mid Range

// Premium
$250 and above

RIDING PANTS

Riding Pants Essential

Riding pants are the most commonly skipped piece of protective gear. Jeans shred in a fraction of a second on asphalt. The knees and hips are the most commonly injured areas in crashes and both are almost entirely unprotected without dedicated riding pants. Modern riding pants look like regular jeans or chinos from a distance — there is no longer any reason to choose between protection and looking normal.

$100 to $200

// Mid Range
$200 to $400

// Premium
$400 and above
Alpinestars GP Plus Leather, REV'IT Sulfur. Race-spec construction for track use and maximum protection.

BOOTS

Boots Essential

Ankles break easily and heal slowly. Motorcycle boots protect against ankle rotation in a crash and impact to the foot and toes. No casual shoe or work boot provides meaningful protection regardless of how sturdy it looks. Like riding pants, modern motorcycle boots have evolved significantly — TCX, Sidi, and Alpinestars all make boots that look like sneakers from a distance but contain full ankle protection systems internally.

// Premium

THE BOTTOM LINE

The helmet comes first and never comes off while you are riding. The gloves come second because hands are always the first thing down. The jacket, the boots, and the pants follow in the order your budget allows. Buy the best you can afford in each category. Wear it every single time. The one ride where you decide not to bother is statistically just as likely to be the one where it matters as any other.
